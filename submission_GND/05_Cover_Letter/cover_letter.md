Dear Editor-in-Chief,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled "A writer-dominant expression state is associated with post-transcriptional spliceosome accumulation in glioblastoma" for consideration as a Full Length Article in Genes & Diseases.

This study addresses a long-standing contradiction in glioma research: transcript-level measurements associate spliceosome expression weakly with outcome, whereas proteomic studies imply the opposite. Through computational integration of multi-omic data, we show that a writer-dominant expression state — quantified by the Writer–Eraser Balance Score (WEBS) — is associated with proteome-wide spliceosome accumulation (107/111 quantified proteins up-regulated) that tracks the state at the protein (Spearman ρ = 0.79, 95% CI 0.68–0.87) but not the mRNA level (ρ = −0.03). The accumulated machinery shows functional-engagement signatures: coordinated acetylation (enrichment retained after parent-protein-abundance correction), SR-protein hyper-phosphorylation with elevated splicing kinases, and 4,387 alternative splicing events that replicate cross-cohort in TCGA-LGG (83% sign-concordant). A six-gene signature derived in TCGA-GBM was externally validated in three independent cohorts (CGGA-693, CGGA-325, GLASS), and convergent computational evidence nominates candidate therapeutic vulnerabilities (MED1, SF3B1, HDAC2, SIRT2).

We emphasize transparency as a central feature of this work. Negative and boundary results — including the attenuation in IDH-wild-type subsets, two underpowered external cohorts, the lack of spliceosome-specific layer discordance under expression-matched permutation control, and patient-level attenuation of some single-cell associations — are reported explicitly throughout. All analyses are fully reproducible: the complete pipeline (60+ scripts) is publicly available at https://github.com/huozheshuashuai/GBM_WEBS, and every figure can be regenerated from the deposited code and public data.

We believe this study fits Genes & Diseases because it connects gene-regulatory imbalance to post-transcriptional proteome remodeling and altered RNA splicing in GBM, provides a reusable computational framework, and delineates testable mechanistic and therapeutic hypotheses with clearly stated evidence boundaries.

This manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration by any other journal.
