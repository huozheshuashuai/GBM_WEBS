Dear Editor-in-Chief,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled "A writer-dominant expression state is associated with post-transcriptional spliceosome accumulation in glioblastoma" for consideration in the British Journal of Cancer.

Glioblastoma remains incurable, and post-transcriptional control of the splicing machinery is an understudied axis of its biology. Reports on splicing machinery and prognosis in glioma are contradictory across measurement layers: transcript-level studies associate spliceosome expression weakly with outcome, whereas proteomic studies imply the opposite. Our study addresses this contradiction directly.

Through computational integration of multi-omic data, we identify a writer-dominant expression state — quantified by the Writer–Eraser Balance Score (WEBS) — that is associated with proteome-wide spliceosome accumulation in GBM (107/111 quantified proteins significantly up-regulated). Critically, this accumulation tracks the state at the protein (ρ = 0.79) but not the mRNA level (ρ = −0.03), and is accompanied by coordinated changes in lysine acetylation and phosphorylation, elevated splicing-kinase abundance, and 4,387 alternative splicing events that replicate in TCGA-LGG with 83% sign concordance. A six-gene signature derived from the associated biology was externally validated in three independent glioma cohorts, and convergent computational evidence nominates candidate therapeutic vulnerabilities (MED1, SF3B1, HDAC2, SIRT2) for experimental follow-up.

We believe this work fits the British Journal of Cancer because it connects gene-regulatory imbalance to a proteome-level remodeling of the RNA-processing machinery in GBM, provides a reusable computational framework (the open WEBS tool, with full pipeline code publicly released), and delineates mechanistic and translational hypotheses with explicitly stated evidence boundaries. Negative and boundary results — including the lack of spliceosome-specific layer discordance under expression-matched permutation, moderate signature discrimination, and attenuation in IDH-wild-type subsets — are reported transparently.

This manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration by any other journal.
