We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled "Post-transcriptional spliceosome accumulation defines a therapeutically targetable malignant cell state in glioblastoma" for consideration as an Original Research article in the Journal of Neuro-Oncology.

Glioblastoma remains the most aggressive primary brain tumor, and precision stratification of its malignant cell states is an unmet clinical need. In this computational translational study, we integrate single-cell transcriptomics (35,823 cells), spatial transcriptomics, CPTAC proteomics and acetylomics, eight clinical cohorts, and pharmacogenomic screens to define a writer-dominant malignant state marked by post-transcriptional spliceosome accumulation — a counterintuitive finding in which spliceosome proteins accumulate without mRNA induction (protein ρ = 0.79 vs mRNA ρ = −0.03). Site-level acetylome analysis nominates acetylation–ubiquitination competition as the stabilizing mechanism.

For the neuro-oncology readership, the study delivers three clinically anchored outputs: (i) a six-gene prognostic signature (HIF1A, SIRT2, MMP2, ITGA5, HDAC1, SPP1) validated in TCGA-GBM and two CGGA cohorts (log-rank p = 0.0028; 3.4 × 10⁻¹⁹; 7.7 × 10⁻¹⁸), suitable for baseline risk stratification of newly diagnosed GBM; (ii) a therapeutically actionable axis — high-WEBS cells show CRISPR dependency on MED1 and spliceosome factors SF3B1/SRSF1 and increased sensitivity to the approved HDAC inhibitors vorinostat and entinostat (q = 1.1 × 10⁻⁴ and 8.7 × 10⁻⁵), with molecular docking supporting target engagement; and (iii) an open tool (WEBS) for quantifying this state in future datasets. We explicitly report where the axis does not predict (immunotherapy response, bulk-RNA survival in TCGA-GBM, non-GBM cancers), framing these as testable boundary hypotheses.

We believe this work fits the Journal of Neuro-Oncology's focus on translational neuro-oncology: it generates concrete, testable hypotheses for GBM precision medicine while openly releasing all code and the analysis pipeline (GitHub + Zenodo DOI) for the community.
