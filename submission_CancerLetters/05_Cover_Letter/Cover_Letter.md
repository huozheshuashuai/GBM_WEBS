# Cover Letter

Dear Editor-in-Chief,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled **"Patient-resolved proteomic evolution of recurrent glioblastoma: a two-axis structure of proliferative-state transition and inflammatory–metabolic remodeling"** for consideration in *Cancer Letters*.

Glioblastoma recurrence has been characterized at the population level, most recently by the longitudinal proteogenomic study of Kim et al. (Cancer Cell, 2024). Whether patients recur through shared or divergent molecular paths — and which recurrence-associated changes are regulatory events versus consequences of cell-state transitions — remains unresolved. Our manuscript addresses these questions with a patient-resolved, error-aware re-analysis of 105 paired primary–recurrence proteomes, and we believe it offers three contributions of interest to your readership:

**First**, we show that inter-patient differences in recurrence evolution are genuine biology rather than technical noise: module-level recurrence deltas vary across patients 7.5- to 260-fold more than technical variance estimated directly from cross-plex duplicate aliquots. Recurrence evolution resolves into two dominant, largely orthogonal axes — a proliferative-to-neuronal axis and an inflammatory–metabolic axis — with patients distributed along a continuum. Notably, near-zero average changes of hypoxia and EMT programs mask strongly bidirectional, patient-divergent evolution, a pattern invisible to population-average analyses.

**Second**, and most importantly, the inflammatory–metabolic axis is robustly validated in a fully independent longitudinal proteome cohort (Tatari et al. 2022; 40 FFPE primary–recurrent pairs on an LFQ platform): the frozen axis projection is significant (p = 2.5 × 10⁻⁴) and robust to module-coverage restriction, exclusion of imputation-suspect proteins, and leave-one-patient-out analysis (40/40 iterations). GeoMx spatial data further show the axis' transcripts are not confined to myeloid-rich regions, supporting a mixed compositional–cellular model of the inflammatory–metabolic program.

**Third**, the study introduces a competing-hypothesis adjudication framework that separates state-coupled from state-unexplained changes — with honest boundary results: no abundance-independent phosphosignaling change was detected at available coverage and power, and no transcript-level support was found in 221 GLASS pairs. We believe this combination of a validated positive finding and rigorously reported boundaries is well aligned with *Cancer Letters*' standards.

This is a secondary analysis of published cohorts; it does not overlap with any submission elsewhere. All analyses were executed and verified by the authors. Code is publicly available at https://github.com/huozheshuashuai/GBM_WEBS.

The manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration by any other journal. All authors have read and approved the manuscript and declare no competing interests.

We hope you find the work suitable for review, and we look forward to your response.
